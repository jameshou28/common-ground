# v3.0 changes

`regions-3.0-v1`, v1 profile, 31 min. The matching machinery (tokens, cell matching, pair model, library weighting) is unchanged. What changed is what the scores are trained on.

## Why

In v2 the terrain score asked "how typical would this Earth block be inside a Moon or Mars region?". The verified analog sites were never used to train it. On held-out data that terrain score ranked verified terrain sites at 43-48% among random land, a coin flip: the library is mostly plains, so flat land matched best, and real analog sites are rugged (median relief 214 m against 48 m for ordinary land). A plain logistic regression on the same measurements, trained on verified sites, ranked them at 11%. So the information was there and the pipeline wasn't using it.

## What changed

- **Terrain head.** Logistic regression on the terrain measurements at both scales: the mean and spread of each cell measurement over the 64 cells, plus the block measurements (66 inputs). Trained on verified terrain analog sites against random land.
  - A site and the 8 blocks 4 km around it share one unit of weight (listed coordinates are often a few km off).
  - GVP volcano blocks and impact-crater blocks count as positives at weight 0.3. Without them the held-out rank is 10.6%, with them 5.1%.
  - Ridge strength picked on held-out areas (1.0).
- **Surface head.** The same kind of model on land cover and rainfall. Snow is separate from bare ground. Signs are fixed where the physics is clear (trees, crops, buildings, water, snow and rain can't help; bare ground can't hurt). The fixed building penalty stays.
- **Region score** = 100 x expit(terrain head + 0.5 x z), where z says how far above ordinary Earth land the block's match to that region is (the normal score of its Earth percentile). The old matching still picks the region, the matched blocks, the wireframe and the landforms. Landform scores work the same way with the landform's own match.
- **Overall** = terrain^0.8 x surface^0.2 (weight picked on held-out areas; always between the two numbers).
- **Held-out by area.** Sites and land in the same 6 degree cell are held out together, 5 folds x 3 splits. Every number below comes from models that never saw the block's area.
- **Antarctica.** WorldCover stops at 60 S. Those blocks are read as no trees, crops, buildings or water, with the bare / ice split unknown (unknown = typical). Before this the Dry Valleys were scored as solid snow, then as typical land.
- **Breakdowns** still add up exactly: starting point (ordinary land) + one term per measurement + `region_match`. `breakdown.kind = "head"` marks the new meaning.
- **Expert list.** `ml/expert_sites.csv`: 21 sites rated before scoring, never used as labels, reported every run.
- Rings (8 blocks at 4 km) are now read for every terrain site, not just the ones with rough coordinates.

Not in: temperature (the WorldClim server was down), Mars ice regions, rock type, permafrost.

## What it still gets wrong

- **Order among good sites.** It separates analog-like land from ordinary land very well; it doesn't rank the good sites the way experts do (Spearman 0.23). Haughton (terrain 28) and Dry Falls (21) are subtle at 30 m, and nothing in the inputs carries cold, permafrost, rock type or impact origin.
- **Which region.** A tagged site's own region is its best region 8% of the time (chance 12.5%). The region part needs its own work: regions competing in training, or ordering by the v2 similarity.
- **Surface is blunt.** Buildings, snow, water and rainfall ended at zero weight (the data pulled them the "wrong" way and the sign limits held them at zero). Svalbard Bockfjorden gets surface 9 at its listed point (84% water).
- **56 training sites.** The heads are small and regularised, but the positives lean to the US Southwest, Iceland and Hawaii.
